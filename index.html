Season Preview

The Year Ahead, Manager by Manager

Ranked by last year's finish. Tap a section below each manager to read how they got there, how they can win it, and what to watch this preseason.

1. Abdullah @bhombalbee

Last year’s champion looks to continue his dominance after his third championship and second in the Sleeper era. Spurred on by a bounce-back season by the undervalued Christian McCaffrey and a solid foundation of Josh Allen, Trey McBride, James Cook, and Ja'Marr Chase, Abdullah strolled to a trophy with victories by 82.8, 39.33, and 60.6 points in the playoff rounds.

A 7-6-1 season did nothing to deter the league’s highest scorer, whose team composed mostly of self-drafted players stood firm throughout the season. The largest trade he completed gave up Davante Adams and trade pieces for a Burrow-less Ja'Marr Chase, who proved he could do it even with old man Flacco at the helm.

Abdullah has already put in work to ensure a good season, with a vacation taken well before the draft allowing him to avoid the pain of learning about post-draft injury woes to his favorite running back. With fantasy gold at every offensive position, the white’s only draft strategy historically employed by this franchise has never been stronger this year. Prudent auction strategy should ensure that Abdullah can field a clan of characters that can keep the winners separate from the losers.

The mystery surrounding Christian McCaffrey’s preseason injury is eerily reminiscent of his disastrous 2024 season, where Abdullah was forced into a subpar 6th place finish without his team’s talisman. If this continues, can Abdullah prove he can click bid on a RB1 with a different name for the first time since 2022?

2. Mitch @mitchhit234

A silver medal for the sophomore manager was somehow a marginal improvement from his third-placed freshman season, showing his skill in a league that he’s widely considered a stranger to. Running almost the exact same roster every week, he battled Singaporean time zones to earn himself a first-round bye before winning a tight semifinal matchup, with a late Monday night touchdown by Jonathan Taylor earning him a finals berth. We all know what happened after, but we can’t forget the success Mitch has had for the second season in a row, especially during a regular season that took 6 weeks to hand him a loss.

A strong draft allowed Mitch to reach the finals with only 2 waiver claims and 1 trade total throughout the season – a trade for the Super Bowl MVP Kenneth Walker and Super TE Bust Jake Ferguson in return for sending away one of the steals of the draft in Javonte Williams. It certainly helps to have the overall WR1 (Puka Nacua) and RB2 (Jonathan Taylor) on your team, producing mostly before one lost his QB and the other gained his Kick followers.

Spending and trading. Ending the season with $169 in FAAB is undesirable by any manager, no matter what your final standing is. If Mitch can somehow use his money to find the one piece that will put him over the edge, he can turn a consistently very good team into one that can win it all and give him a reason to be up at 3AM on a Monday morning.

Another year in Singapore puts Mitch further and further away from American sports and the hype of an NFL preseason. How much does he still know and how much knowledge has he lost? Has a Wembanyama-esque trip to China inspired newfound competitive edge in Mitch, or has it given him a disease with which the symptoms are to only draft players numbered 88?

3. Louis @FobAsian

Third place for a player that squeaked into the playoffs by 1.22 points seems like a miracle, but a slow start and late season slump concealed a dangerous team full of boom-bust players. Patient management and a willingness to bench players that were “must-drafts” allowed Louis to weather some rough weeks from Emeka Egbuka and AJ Brown and rely on the talent of the undervalued D'Andre Swift and Luther Burden while capitalizing on the return of the speedy Rashee Rice. He didn’t win any moral victory points, but does that matter when you’re within 4 points reach of another finals appearance?

Patience, patience, patience. As frustrating as it can be, Louis’s confidence in his guys no matter what the circumstance allowed him to weather the worst of a troubled AJ Brown season and a suspension for Rashee Rice, starting them in big games later in the season where they gained valuable points in a fight for the last playoff seed. Louis also impressively found value where others didn’t, with impressive and consistent quarterback and kicker play allowing his team to have a floor for his boom-bust players to thrive. Not to mention the drafting of a certain Bijan Robinson, whose acquisition earned him further jealousy and hatred of a manager who he’s only been successful against in the league’s history.

With the league’s highest playoff appearances without a trophy, Louis’s consistency might be his biggest obstacle in his path to a championship. A team devoid of top-10 players at any position besides Bijan is going to struggle in the playoffs, no doubts about it. However, things are trending up for Louis. A willingness to spend big on players and take risks landed him Bijan and Rashee Rice, and you could argue things are looking up even more for the season ahead. With a new job and money to spend in real life, will that mentality spill into fantasy football and allow Louis to continue his upward trajectory?

The fantasy football draft will be held in person this year, with his rival Minh in attendance. That means Louis will have to endure hours of temptation drafting players that Minh calls out as “value” and further talks of a “spreadsheet”. Will he fall for the bait, or will he draft the boom-bust team that takes him to the promised land?

4. Mason @kvkv

A respectable finish from a player who was, before the season started, a large question mark for attendance in the first place. One of the founding members of the league, his absence would have marked the first season with as many non-founding members as founding members. Thankfully, Mason was able to participate in a season that saw him as the first seed coming into the playoffs. Luck and variance was on his side for the season (he was the fourth-ranked team by average weekly points), and I’m sure he will be the first to admit that his demolition in the playoffs was, in his words, “not so lucky”.

A solid wide receiver core of notable meatmatician Amon-Ra St. Brown and target-peppered Drake London was supported by a cast straight out of a Dutch royal family: three Williams holding the line. Javonte, Kyren, and Jameson Williams provided a floor that led to the most statistically consistent playoff team in the league and allowed him to win weeks he shouldn’t have and avoid losses in the weeks he might have lost.

With no questions about his participation this year and a recipient of the newborn baby buff, Mason’s focus on this season is in no question and he should be back with a point to prove. In what was his first winning season since 2018, a taste of success should be all he needs to bring back his first championship.

Will Mason be able to find another last name to structure his team around? The Williams strategy might have worked last year, but with the rise of more unique last names throughout the league Mason may have to pivot tactics. Perhaps a food-based scheme with players like Makai Lemon, CeeDee Lamb, Rashee Rice, and Tucker Kraft could prove successful instead.

5. Minh @Minhtyfreshh

In what can only be considered a heartbreak for the second-highest scoring manager, this team lost to Louis in a shootout to a 44-point performance from last minute QB replacement Trevor Lawrence. To put it into perspective, Minh’s team only trailed Abdullah’s weekly points average by 5, 125-120, while outscoring the league average by 12 points. But no one cares about a good regular season if it busts in your face during the playoffs, and Minh will look to avoid anything of the sort in a season that brings a fresh start.

By somehow having Jahmyr Gibbs, Devon Achane, Brock Bowers, Derrick Henry, Nico Collins, and Matt Stafford on the same team. Just looking back at that roster makes me wet in the mouth. However, throughout most of the season this roster was never able to put it all together. Sometimes it was a QB that went into the negatives, sometimes a K, most of the time a DST. And missing out on a first round bye turned a championship contender to an instant first round exit – showing how fine the margins are for winning in this league.

By sticking to his biggest strength – fleecing teammates. Minh completed a whopping 12 trades last season, in a league where the median amount of trades completed by a team is 4. He also led the league with 27 waiver claims, including late season heroes like the previously-mentioned Stafford and Michael Wilson. The recruitment of new league member Michael K only provides an additional manager for Minh to fleece, which other managers will be wary of. Expect any trade proposal from Minh to be met with a side eye and a skeptical response, most likely an auto-reject.

By his own admission, this will be the season Minh has entered with the least preparation beforehand. No advanced statistics on a spreadsheet, and barely any total hours on fantasy podcasts or team analyses. Couple this with a flight back to Texas for his first in-person draft in years, and experts are unsure if Minh can recreate the magic of trades, draft hits, and waiver finds that led to his success last year.

6. Peter @peterluong

Once considered the “Madden Rating drafter” and the “Prosper feeder team”, Peter has found himself to be the recipient of two championships in the Sleeper era and $395 in money towards student loans. The youngest manager in the league, Peter was able to find himself another successful season where he recorded the first tie in league history – a 107.07 stalemate between himself and the eventual champion Abdullah.

A strong start straight out of the draft with Lamar Jackson, Ja'Marr Chase, Ashton Jeanty, and George Pickens fell off the wheels when injuries and trades let a promising season fall off the rails. If not for the tie with Abdullah, Peter would have been in the mix for the final seed with a point total only 6.5 above Louis.

To be honest, you somehow can never rule Peter out of winning it no matter what happens. His 2021 championship came thanks to a 22.6 point game from Boston Scott of all people. To put that into perspective, Boston Scott would barely break a total of 50 fantasy points for the rest of his career. His 2020 championship was famously due to the Kamarageddon game, a 55 point boom week that made it impossible to lose if you had him in your finals team. Both victories came at the cost of a Tony second place finish, so maybe Peter’s path to a championship depends on Tony’s success as well.

According to IGN, the newest Madden entry is actually good for once. Maybe the specter of Kyler Murray will take over Peter and his affinity for the Madden release clouds his judgement of fantasy production. Draft Matthew Stafford as your QB1 Peter, he’s a 99 overall.

7. Prosper @prosper

The defending champion from 2024, Prosper can consider his seventh place finish a disappointing result with the league’s return to the Auction Draft format. And looking at the draft, it isn’t hard to see why – he immediately spent $175 of his $200 budget on three players – Saquon Barkley, Derrick Henry, and Chase Brown. The rest of the team comprised of a patchwork of “budget” players that held up to an extent but never allowed him to reach the boom weeks needed to win in this league. Out of the 14 weeks played in the regular season, he only broke 100 points 5 times, tied-last with Thomas. However, he brought it back during the toilet bowl and ended with a respectable best-of-the-rest ranking.

We sadly cannot look at the context of Prosper’s season without mentioning the elephant in the room. In Week 7 of the season and after days of discussion, a blockbuster trade was made. Perennial 1000-yard receiver Mike Evans and JAG Jordan Mason would be traded to Prosper from Minh, for a return of star RB Derrick Henry and consistent floor play Michael Pittman. Even though the trade was completely fair, it was widely considered a fleece by the league at the time with Minh getting the better end of the deal. It would get worse from there, as in the first half of a game in which he had 0 points, Mike Evans would fracture his collarbone and be ruled out for the rest of the season. To this day, Minh feels bad about the trade, and I’m sure if he could take it back at the time he wouldn’t.

The stars and studs strategy is a tried and true tactic, but Prosper may have taken it a bit over the edge last year. Maybe the draft day food was too good and he had to lock in and eat. Prosper has proved in the past that, if he gets his guys, his team can be unstoppable. Some more work in the waiver wire, and a full trade embargo on Minh might be what he needs to get back to the levels he knows he can be.

As of the writing of this preview, the league is still drafting in Auction Mode and does not seem to have to resort to Snake Draft. This is a huge debuff to the snake draft expert, who has never finished above seventh in that format. 2026 will be a big test for Prosper, but with the employment of the new dad debuff for the third time, there’s hope for a big comeback.

8. Max @maximo1237

To be honest, I look at Max’s team and am surprised he didn’t do better last year. Seventh place in points for, missing playoffs by a 1.22 point margin, and overall standing of 8 after the toilet bowl had to be underwhelming for a team that had made 3 playoffs in a row. Given his track record as a manager that's steadily improved in his time with this league, you can bet he's ready to bounce back and continue that upward trajectory with a new season and a New York football team that won't completely suck.

Holding true to his team name of 0% RBs, 100% Heart, max struggled with production at the position all season. Injuries and poor play from Alvin Kamara meant that a trade for Travis Etienne was the only thing besides the breakout of Jaxon Smith-Njigba that could keep his team afloat. Max’s team reminds me a lot of a Great Value version of Louis’s team – huge faith in his guys except his guys actually kinda sucked. A platoon of Mahomes and Mayfield and a hold of Bucky Irving just didn’t give the oomph that this team needed to get ahead, and the result was a playoff race heartbreak.

The opposite of the Louis strategy, in my opinion. Max had some nice offers come in and could have offloaded his middling players for decent returns, but a refusal to do so until it was too late was his downfall. The one trade he was able to make – Emeka Egbuka for Travis Etienne – was fantastic for his team construction. Max has the ability to build a good team, that I’m confident about. It’ll just be a matter of if he can let go of his guys. Maybe he should draft some unscrupulous characters to make it easier or rewatch Moneyball.

Last week Max drafted while in the middle of a tennis match, which does not sound like the most optimal of draft environments. This year he might be drafting at an airport or hopefully at home – both much better fantasy football draft environments. The election of Zohran Mamdani should also be a huge lift for the New York sports fan, who can always rely on the help of the populist mayor in case things go sour during the season.

9. Tony @myliltony

“’My name is Ozymandias, king of kings: Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!’ Nothing beside remains.” The fall of a king is a sight to behold, both positively and negatively. And Tony holds a special position in the league as a once-unstoppable fantasy veteran who shocked the league with the novel strategy of “Drafting running-backs” during the inaugural season. With the league’s all time highest win percentage for members with more than 1 season coming into 2025, his 4-10 finish marked the second year in a row where he did not make playoffs.

His running back core was Omarion Hampton and TreVeyon Henderson. Not much else to be said there. A big bet on two promising rookies ruined by a season-ending injury to one and a season-enduring dookie by the other meant subpar running back play for a whole season, coupled with $60 spent on Justin Jefferson only to find out his worth was closer to $9. He never stopped trying, making several trades that were a win at the time but in hindsight turned him into Minh’s B team.

I can never picture Tony not being a favorite to win no matter what happens. The dude just lives and breathes fantasy and will never stop trying to improve his team no matter what the circumstances are. I’m sure a pivot in draft strategy along with some luck regression™ going his way can immediately drop him to favorites-territory. Plus, he is not scheduled to work on that day, meaning he will definitely not accidentally draft both Rams wide receivers back to back.

Like Lebron moving to a team without Bronny, Thomas’s hiatus from the league marks the first time since 2019 where Tony is without sibling in the league. How will he deal with the pressure of carrying the Vu name on his own?

10. Michael K. @michaelburps NEW

Not to be confused with former league regular Michael Y, Michael K provides a level of variance and mystery to the league that hasn’t seen a new member since the addition of Mitch in 2024. A San Francisco native and lifelong 49ers fan, his performance in the league will be something to look out for. In the next segment, I caught up with the new member and asked him to give a quick introduction to himself.

Interview with Michael

Where are you from, and where are you now?

Michael, from Cupertino, California — living in Brooklyn, NY.

What team do you support?

Pittsburgh Steelers, babyyyyyy.

How'd your fantasy football teams do last year?

Who's the biggest draft bust you've ever drafted?

Kyle Pitts. (This year is his year.)

Whoever drafts the most Chicago Bears wins it. Whoever drafts the fewest finishes last.

Favorite sports memory?

Madison Bumgarner pitching Game 7 of the 2014 World Series.

If you could play any sport professionally, which would you choose?

F1 driver or esports — I want to be sitting down.
